## Layout axis — the first unconfounded comparison, and it changes the picture

| layout | OLD rms_px | NEW rms_px | OLD recon_mm | NEW recon_mm |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| grid | 0.708139 | 0.775560 | 0.211043 | 0.538454 |
| ring | 0.708456 | 0.704959 | 0.212163 | **0.717436** |
| line | *failed* | 0.789925 | *failed* | 0.541051 |

**The committed layout rows were confounded and these are not.** Under the old geometry each
layout received a different board-to-array alignment (0.18 m for `grid`, 0.19 m for `ring`,
0.55 m for `line`) and a different fraction of its own footprint covered. A `ring` row reading
differently from `grid` may have been measuring a centroid offset. D-27 and D-28 removed both.

**What the numbers show** (reported, not adjudicated — D-12 stands, no verdict column added):
old `ring` and `grid` were indistinguishable in reconstruction (0.2122 vs 0.2110, 0.5%).
Unconfounded, `ring` is **33% worse** than `grid` in reconstruction (0.7174 vs 0.5385) while
being *better* in reprojection RMS (0.7050 vs 0.7756). The confounded comparison hid a real
difference between layouts, and the direction of that difference is invisible to reprojection
RMS — the two metrics rank the layouts oppositely.

> **Do not read the OLD-vs-NEW reconstruction column as a regression.** Old ~0.19–0.21 mm and
> new ~0.54 mm are different scenes, not better and worse solves: D-29 moved the rig from
> 0.15 m to 1.031 m above water with a 1.1–2.0 m depth range, so a millimetre error is measured
> over a volume several times larger. Cross-geometry absolute comparison is meaningless here;
> only within-table comparison is valid.

## Index axis — 8/8 ok, and what it does NOT mean

| index | 1.333 | 1.36 | 1.39 | 1.42 | 1.45 | 1.48 | 1.51 | 1.55 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| rms_px | 0.7756 | 0.7809 | 0.7679 | 0.7888 | 0.7056 | 0.7047 | 0.7055 | 0.7052 |
| recon_mm | 0.5385 | 0.5357 | 0.5528 | 0.5704 | 0.5550 | 0.5483 | 0.5406 | 0.5441 |

Accuracy is flat across water (1.333) → resin (1.55): reconstruction varies 6% over a 16%
change in index, with no monotone trend.

> **This axis varies the MEDIUM, not the assumed index.** Each cell is a self-consistent
> scenario at a different refractive index, and the axis exists to demonstrate the
> arbitrary-index-ratio claim — that AquaCal's refractive model is not tuned to water. Flat
> performance is therefore the *intended* result and supports that claim.
>
> It is **not** a misspecification test, and must not be reported as "reprojection error is
> insensitive to index error." Index *misspecification* sensitivity is E5's subject. This
> distinction was misread once during this run before being checked against
> `INDEX_AXIS_VALUES`' definition.

## Provenance (EXP-11) — complete, with one recorded inconsistency

All 12 checkpoints and `e6_provenance.json` carry `schema_version`, `seed` (42),
`solver_config["seed"]` (42), and an `environment` block with `aquacal_version` 1.8.0 and
`git_sha`. The `seed` column is populated on all 14 CSV rows.

> **DEFECT IN THE PROVENANCE RECORD, not in the science.** The checkpoints do not agree on
> `git_sha`: `baseline.json` records `3d5c3e6`, the other **11 record `3d0aa3e`**. The cause is
> that a documentation commit (`3d0aa3e`, the D-36 decision) was made **while the sweep was
> running**, and `capture_environment` calls `git rev-parse` per configuration at run time.
>
> **The code was identical across both SHAs** — `git diff --name-only 3d5c3e6 3d0aa3e -- src/
> experiments/` is empty; the diff is two `.planning/` files. So no cell ran different code from
> any other, and the measurements are sound. What is damaged is the artifact's ability to name
> one commit as the code that produced it.
>
> **Lesson: plan 22 forbids modifying `src/` or `experiments/` during the run, and that was
> honoured — but the prohibition is too narrow.** Any commit at all perturbs recorded
> provenance, because the SHA is captured per configuration rather than once at launch.
> Two candidate fixes for a later plan: capture the environment once at sweep start and stamp
> every checkpoint from it, or record the launch SHA alongside the per-cell one. Neither is
> made here — this wave changes no code.
