## D-27 — `generate_board_trajectory` centres the volume on the array centroid, by default

**Decision.** Add a `center` parameter to `generate_board_trajectory`
(`src/aquacal/datasets/synthetic.py`). It defaults to **the centroid of
`camera_positions`**, NOT to the origin and NOT to today's behaviour.

**This is a deliberate NON-INERT library change.** It is the one place in this phase
where inertness is explicitly not the goal.

**Why.** Today the function samples `x = uniform(-xy_extent, +xy_extent)` about the
**origin**, while `generate_camera_array` recentres the array so **cam0** sits at the
origin. cam0 is a *corner* camera under `grid` and an *end* camera under `line` — never
the middle. The calibration volume is therefore pinned to a corner of the rig, by an
amount that differs per layout:

| layout (12 cams) | array span | centroid | offset from board volume |
|---|---|---|---|
| grid | +0.00…+0.30 | (+0.15, +0.10) | 0.18 m |
| ring | −0.38…+0.00 | (−0.19, 0.00) | 0.19 m |
| line | +0.00…+1.10 | (+0.55, 0.00) | **0.55 m** |

The board volume is (0,0) ± 0.15 m in all three cases. Under `grid`, half that volume
lies at negative x where no camera exists. Under `line`, the board never leaves the first
~14% of the rig, which is the actual cause of the `KeyError: 'cam11'` recorded in the
committed `generalization_sweep.csv`.

**The function already receives `camera_positions` as a required argument** and simply
does not use it for centring. `generate_real_rig_trajectory` — the realistic path — solves
this correctly and explicitly (`X_CENTER, Y_CENTER = -0.34, 0.55`, commented "Center of the
rig footprint... Board should move throughout this area to ensure all cameras see it").
The grid-family generator never received that fix. There is no scenario in which centring
the calibration volume on an arbitrary corner camera is the desired behaviour, so the
default is corrected rather than preserved.

**Consequence accepted by the user:** some experiments are pre-update and some post-update.
That is acceptable **only** because the containment audit below shows no committed result
depends on the changed path.

## D-28 — `xy_extent` scales with the array footprint

**Decision.** The grid family derives `xy_extent` from the array's span rather than using
a fixed 0.15 m, with a floor so small arrays still get a usable volume.

**Why.** `xy_extent` is fixed at 0.15 m whether the array spans 0.20 m or 1.10 m. The
realistic generator uses ±0.7 m against a ~1.3 m footprint — about **±0.54× the span**.
The grid family ranges from ±0.75× down to ±0.14×, so each layout exercises a different
fraction of its own footprint.

**Why this matters beyond the `line` crash.** Together with D-27, this means E6's layout
axis is currently **confounded**: `grid`, `ring` and `line` each get a different
board-to-array alignment and a different fraction of footprint covered. A `ring` row that
reads slightly worse than `grid` may be measuring a 0.19 m centroid offset rather than
anything about ring geometry. The committed layout rows are therefore not trustworthy as a
layout comparison — a separate and more serious problem than board overfill, which at
least applies uniformly across cells.

## D-29 — The grid family moves to realistic optical geometry

**Decision.** `build_grid_scenario`'s defaults become approximately
`height_above_water = 1.031`, `depth_range = (1.1, 2.0)`, `spacing ≈ 0.43`.
Exact values are the planner's to finalise; the acceptance property is that
**the board fits within the frame at every depth in the range**.

**Why.** Today the grid family sits **0.15 m** above the water with a 0.30 m footprint,
while the real rig and the `realistic` preset both sit at **~1.03 m** with a ~1.34 m
footprint. The 0.72 × 0.54 m board — the same physical board in both cases, deliberately
not scaled — **overfills the frame** in the grid family:

| geometry | view at working depth | board visible |
|---|---|---|
| grid family (0.15 m) | 0.29 × 0.17 → 0.54 × 0.31 m | **12.8% → 43.1%** (measured 22% mean) |
| real rig (1.031 m) | 1.09 × 1.73 m | **100%** |

Verified proposal at `height=1.031`, `depth=(1.1, 2.0)`:

| depth | view | board fits | adjacent overlap @ spacing 0.43 |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1.1 m | 1.25 × 0.70 m | ✓ | 25% |
| 1.5 m | 1.57 × 0.89 m | ✓ | 38% |
| 2.0 m | 1.97 × 1.13 m | ✓ | 49% |

Overlap stays in the same band as today's 27–55%, so redundancy is preserved.

**What was checked and found NOT to be wrong.** The current array is not degenerate: 40–52%
of cameras see the board each frame (5.4–7.0 cameras, not the bare minimum of 2), adjacent
overlap is 27–55% by area, and `validation_3d_error_mean` is ~1e-6 m with ~0.17 mm std at
0.70 px reprojection against 0.5 px noise. The justification for the change is
**representativeness**, not infeasibility: E4 and E6 exist to characterise cost and
generalisation for deployments like the real rig, and at 22% corner visibility the residual
counts that drive memory and runtime describe no rig anyone would build.

**Nothing published moves.** E4/E6 feed reviewer responses R1.4/R1.5/R3.2, not §3. E1, E5
and E7 already run at real-rig geometry. See `MANUSCRIPT-FINDINGS.md` MF-02.

### Expect the 16×200 cell to OOM

If corners per view rise from 19.5 toward 88, residuals scale 3–4.5× and peak memory
projects to **9.9–14.9 GiB** against a ~15.7 GiB box:

| corner-density rise | residuals | projected peak | projected stage 3 |
|---|---|---|---|
| 2.0× | 108,920 | ~6.6 GiB | ~5 min |
| 3.0× | 163,380 | ~9.9 GiB | ~8 min |
| 4.5× | 245,070 | ~14.9 GiB | ~12 min |

16×200 is **pre-authorised to fail** as a recorded row, exactly as in plan 19.2-09.
Escalate only if a 16-camera cell **other than** 16×200 fails, since the curve needs at
least one successful 16-camera point. Note the irony worth recording in the summary: the
cell only fit at 3.31 GiB *because* the geometry was unrealistic; the original ~14.4 GiB
projection was closer to correct than the measurement that appeared to refute it.

## D-30 — W1 lands before W3 (ordering constraint)

Because D-29 makes an OOM likely, the failed-cell and resume paths stop being theoretical
and become load-bearing. CR-01/02/03 MUST be fixed before the re-run:

- **CR-01 / CR-02** — E4 (`e4_benchmark_grid.py:838`) and E6
  (`e6_generalization_sweep.py:463-469`) write per-cell/per-config records so an
  interrupted run can resume, then **discard those records' metrics on the resume path and
  publish an all-null CSV**. Each module's own `--check` path already does the correct
  reconstitution a few lines away. Not currently caught by any test — every E4 fixture uses
  `status="ok"`. The committed CSVs are unaffected only because every production run used
  `--force`.
- **CR-03** — E4's aggregation dies after all nine cells have solved if E2's
  `benchmark.json` is missing or the cwd differs (unguarded `open()` at `:843`, cwd-relative
  `E2_BENCHMARK_PATH` at `:162`), losing both the CSV and the `.tex`. E3's equivalent read
  degrades gracefully; match that.
- **CR-04** — `compare_experiment_csv` still crashes. Reproduced by the orchestrator:
  `ValueError: Can only compare identically-labeled Series objects` on a row-count mismatch,
  and `ValueError: could not convert string to float: "KeyError: 'cam11'"` when a column is
  float-in-fresh but string-in-committed. This is the **third** bug in this function this
  phase (`ee8af31`, `ac75e35`), each previously patched by analogy to the last. Fix the
  dtype-reconciliation contract generally rather than adding a third special case; a
  row-count mismatch must produce a report, not an exception.

`SEEDLESS_LEGACY_RECORDS` is **not** part of this problem — it was audited and holds up:
exactly six members, each asserted to exist *and* to still genuinely lack a seed (so it

## D-32 — CR-05: the Newton diagnostic measures a loop production never runs

`refractive_project_newton_diagnostic` (`core/refractive_geometry.py:467`) documents itself
as performing "the identical root-find as `_refractive_project_newton` — the shipped
projection path used inside every residual evaluation", via the shared `_solve_newton_r_p`.
Confirmed false in source: `calibration/_optim_common.py:635` projects through
`refractive_project_batch` (`:718`) → `_refractive_project_newton_batch` (`:546`), which
contains its own hand-inlined Newton loop, never calls `_solve_newton_r_p` (grep count 0),
and terminates on `np.all(np.abs(delta) < tolerance)` — all points at once, with no
per-point convergence flag.

So `newton_iterations.csv`'s published `n_not_converged = 0` is a property of a loop the
optimizer never runs, and its per-point iteration counts do not transfer: under all-points
termination every point iterates until the slowest converges.

**This is the sharpest form of the goal's traceability clause failing** — provenance
pointing at the wrong implementation is worse than absent provenance, because it is citable.
It also bears on `MANUSCRIPT-FINDINGS.md` MF-01, which now carries a resolve-before-citing
caveat.

**Requires a user decision** (do not let a planner choose silently):
- **(a)** migrate `_refractive_project_newton_batch` onto `_solve_newton_r_p` — removes the
  third duplicate loop the helper was created to prevent, but changes the production
  projection path and must be proven numerically inert; or
- **(b)** correct the docstring and restate the CSV's scope as the scalar path, leaving
  three Newton implementations in the tree.
