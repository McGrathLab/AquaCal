Entry point: `python -u -m experiments.e6_generalization_sweep --force`
  Runs every entry of `build_axis_configurations()`, computing the shared baseline scene once and
  reusing its result for all three baseline rows, and writes `generalization_sweep.csv` with
  columns `E6_COLUMNS`, keyed on `["axis", "axis_value"]`.

Per-configuration resumability (review M7): each configuration writes its own small JSON under
`<out>/e6_configs/{config_key}.json` BEFORE the next begins, and is skipped on a later run unless
`--force` is set. The CSV is assembled from those files at the end. An interrupted sweep therefore
resumes per configuration rather than restarting — which matters most here, because E6 is both the
largest sweep in the phase and the designated calendar-fallback casualty. `write_experiment_csv`'s
own skip-unless-`force` remains FILE-level and applies only to the final CSV.

Expected outcomes, all recorded rather than chased (D-12, RESEARCH Pitfall 4):
  - a `ring` or `line` layout at 12 cameras may produce a disconnected pose graph and fail
    Stage 2, giving `status=failed` with a reason;
  - accuracy may genuinely degrade at n = 1.55 or on a line layout. R1.4 asked whether the method
    generalizes; a bounded, characterized degradation is a better answer than a claim of universal
    invariance a reviewer can poke.
