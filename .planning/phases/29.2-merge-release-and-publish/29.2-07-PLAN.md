<objective>
Take the two archive doors in their required order: publish the results record after its rendered
description has been read end to end, then give the inputs record a structured pointer at the newly
minted identifier — and record that publication preceded submission, which is what closes the
requirement.

Purpose: this is the outward-facing half of the phase's requirement and the last thing standing
between the work and the paper's data-availability claim. Phase 29 built and verified both records
and deliberately stopped short of publishing, because the irreversible act is a human one. The
ordering is not a convenience: the results record's prose names a version, which had to exist first;
the inputs record's pointer is the results record's identifier, which does not exist until it is
published.

Output: the two published-and-linked records, and the evidence file section that closes the
requirement on ordering.
</objective>

before the version it names existed would have been false at the moment of minting, and no later edit
repairs that. This ordering has been satisfied.

digest is recorded; the version named in its prose is the released version; the supersession
