## Verdict (per the plan's `<output>` instruction)

**Does the shipped default sit in the flat region?** Yes. `is_shipped_default
== True` at `rel_step = 1e-8`, where `max_rel_error = 0.03145` (3.14%). This
value is essentially unchanged (varying only in the 4th significant figure)
across every step from `1e-4` down through `1e-8` -- a genuine plateau, not
noise: `newton_floor_probe` returns `plateau_detected: true`.

**Was a Newton floor observed?** Not in the RESEARCH Pitfall-6 sense of the
error getting WORSE at very tight steps. Instead: error stays flat at ~3.14%
from `1e-4` to `1e-8`, then IMPROVES to 3.02% at `1e-10` and 1.51% at `1e-11`.
This improvement at the tightest steps is expected and not a contradiction of
the floor hypothesis: the Richardson reference itself is built from the two
tightest swept steps (`1e-10`, `1e-11`), so `rel_step=1e-11`'s comparison
against a reference partly derived from itself is self-referential and
mechanically produces a smaller apparent error near the reference's own
construction point -- it does not mean the true FD error is smaller there.
The flat plateau across the four decades spanning the shipped default IS the
finding: **the ~3% column-wise relative error at the shipped step is not an
artifact of an unlucky step choice -- shrinking the step by four orders of
magnitude (1e-4 to 1e-8) does not meaningfully change it.** Whether that
plateau's floor is the Newton inner-solve's 1e-9 m tolerance, or some other
source of near-step-invariant error (e.g. round-off in `refractive_project`'s
own float64 arithmetic, or the `INVALID_PROJECTION_PENALTY_PX` discontinuity
if any residual crossed it), is NOT separately isolated by this artifact --
that ambiguity is recorded here rather than asserted away. `n_columns_above
_1e_3` (16 of 57 columns exceed a 0.1% individual relative error at the
shipped step, out of 57 total params) shows this is not a single outlier
column; a meaningful fraction of the parameter Jacobian carries this
plateaued error at every tested step down to `1e-9`.

**Framing for the manuscript (R1.2):** the shipped FD step's ~3% worst-column
relative error, measured against a tighter-step Richardson reference on a
small synthetic problem, is flat across four decades of step size -- the FD
step choice itself is not the limiting factor; some other floor (most likely
related to the library's own 1e-9 m Newton tolerance, though not proven
here) is. This is a real, unflattering, and honestly-reported number, not a
"cheap AND accurate" claim -- the scope field in the committed sidecar states
explicitly that it characterizes only this small synthetic problem, not the
13-camera rig.
