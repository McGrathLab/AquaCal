<design_decision>
**Solve COV-02 entirely inside `experiments/`.** D-19.5-03 permits a library hook only under the
D-32 precedent, and PATTERNS.md records what that precedent actually is: a *separately named*
diagnostic function sitting beside an untouched production call site
(`refractive_project_newton_diagnostic`), never a boolean threaded into a hot path. COV-02 needs
neither — `approx_derivative` is a public scipy call and the cost function, sparsity pattern and
column groups are all importable. Build both Jacobians in `experiments/`, call the production
functions read-only, and change nothing in `src/aquacal/`. If during execution you find a genuine
blocker to this, **stop and report it**; do not add a flag to `src/`.

**Reference Jacobian: two-step Richardson, computed with the same `approx_derivative` call.**
Comparing against a hand-rolled central difference would measure an implementation difference
rather than a step-size difference. Use `approx_derivative` twice at two relative steps `h` and
`h/2` and combine, so the only thing that varies is the step.

**Expect a floor, and report it.** RESEARCH Pitfall 6: because the residual passes through a
Newton solve converged only to 1e-9 m, the error curve is expected to stop improving — and go
non-monotonic — at small steps. That plateau is the finding, not noise to average away. Under
D-19.5-07 a flat or ugly curve is a reportable result.
</design_decision>

      between the Gauss-Newton steps the two Jacobians induce for the same residual vector, so

      matrices different". Use a least-squares solve; do not form a normal-equations inverse.
    - `newton_floor_probe(rel_steps, errors) -> dict` — detects a plateau or non-monotonicity in
      the error-vs-step curve and returns `{"plateau_detected": bool,
      "first_non_monotonic_step": float | None}`. This is what surfaces the 1e-9 Newton floor
      rather than swallowing it.
