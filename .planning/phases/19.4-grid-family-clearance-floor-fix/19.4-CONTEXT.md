<domain>
## Phase Boundary

Make every grid-family scenario derive its depth range from its own `water_zs` at the point the
scenario is constructed, so E4 and E6 are legal at any seed — **without moving a single
published number.**

**In scope:** the six call sites enumerated in the diagnosis (`e4_benchmark_grid.py:258`, `:589`,
`:744`, `:806`; `e6_generalization_sweep.py:160`, and its two `run_configuration` calls at `:759`
and `:787`); the checkpoint-identity and `E6_COLUMNS` consequences of changing the depth-range
payload; the two tests that encode the current design; a legality regression test whose absence
is why this shipped.

**Out of scope:** `board_clearance_floor`'s `margin_factor` (its docstring forbids moving it in
response to a failing run, and the failing thing is not the margin); the refractive model; E1,
E2, E3, E5 and E7 (the defect is confined to the grid family — every other call site already
derives its range from its own scenario's `water_zs`, which is why E1 and E7 swept ×10 cleanly);
re-deriving `WATER_Z`; regenerating any published artifact.

**Explicitly NOT in scope: changing any published number.** The published seed-42 E4 grid and E6
sweep are not in question and must not be re-litigated. If the fix moves them, the fix is wrong.

</domain>

<decisions>
## Implementation Decisions

### Settled by measurement — do not reopen

- **D-19.4-01: The rule is per-scenario derivation, never a module-level constant.**
  A scenario's depth range must be derived from the `water_zs` that scenario was built with, at
  construction time. `e5_index_sensitivity.py`'s `_e5_real_rig_depth_range(water_zs)` is the
  pattern to copy. This is the phase's entire thesis.

- **D-19.4-02: The fix form is `max(GRID_DEPTH_RANGE[0], board_clearance_floor(...))`.
  `depth_range=None` is FORBIDDEN as the remedy.**

  > ⚠ The guard's own error message recommends `depth_range=None`. **It is not inert.** It is
  > bit-identical for the calibration scene but shifts the *holdout* scene's board depths by up
  > to **0.469 mm** across 100 frames, which would silently move every published E4 and E6
  > held-out accuracy number. Measured, not predicted. An earlier note claiming the fix was
  > "verified inert at seed 42" covered only the calibration scenario and is retracted.

  The `max()` form is legal by construction at every seed (never below the derived floor) and
  equal to the frozen constant wherever the derived floor is lower — which covers all six
  seed-42 grid arrays. Verified bit-identical on all 100 poses' `rvec` and `tvec`.
  **`GRID_DEPTH_RANGE` must therefore NOT be deleted** — it is the published-geometry anchor and
  the lower bound of the `max()`. Deleting it breaks seed-42 inertness. Rename and re-document
  it so no new consumer reads it as "the floor".

### Decided — locked 2026-08-04, do not reopen without a new decision record

- **D-19.4-03 (APPROVED 2026-08-04): record the RESOLVED ABSOLUTE range in the
  checkpoint payload, so `_SCENARIO_IDENTITY_KEYS` needs no migration.**

  `_SCENARIO_IDENTITY_KEYS` contains `"depth_range"`. The diagnosis proposes threading a
  relative `depth_extent_factor` through `build_grid_scenario`, which changes the payload's
  *shape* — and then **every committed `e6_configs/*.json` mismatches under `--check` (WR-03)**
  unless the key set and all recorded identities migrate together.

  There is a cheaper option that falls out of D-19.4-02's inertness property. Thread the factor
  *internally* (the scale axis genuinely needs one), but keep recording `depth_range` in the
  payload as the **resolved absolute tuple**, so no key set changes and `--check` keeps working
  on the published artifacts by construction.

  > **⚠ CORRECTED 2026-08-04 during planning, after measurement.** The paragraph above
  > originally claimed that recording the resolved tuple would leave "every committed identity
  > matching byte-for-byte." **That was wrong.** Reading the twelve committed checkpoints under
  > `experiments/results/e6_configs/` shows **ten of them record `"depth_range": null`** — only
  > `scale_half_scale` and `scale_double_scale` carry tuples:
  >
  > ```
  > baseline, index_1.36 … 1.55, layout_line, layout_ring  →  null          (10)
  > scale_half_scale    → [1.181852154281008, 1.590926077140504]
  > scale_double_scale  → [1.181852154281008, 2.818147845718992]
  > ```
  >
  > Recording a resolved tuple *unconditionally* would flip ten nulls to values and mismatch
  > 10/12 — reintroducing exactly the migration this decision exists to avoid.
  >
  > **The corrected form, which achieves the decision's actual goal:** record the resolved
  > absolute tuple **where the configuration declares a non-default extent factor**, and `None`
  > where it declares the default. That reproduces all twelve committed payloads at seed 42.
  > Verified: `floor + 0.5×extent` and `floor + 2.0×extent` reproduce both committed scale
  > tuples exactly in IEEE-754, so no special case is needed. Plan 19.4-03 carries a mandatory
  > test asserting 12/12.
  >
  > The "what depth range was actually used" information still reaches a human — via the CSV
  > columns under D-19.4-04, which is where it belongs and where it is actually read.
  >
  > *Recorded rather than silently edited: the decision's conclusion (no identity migration)
  > survives, but the reasoning that justified it was factually wrong and the implementation
  > differs.*

  **Tradeoff, stated honestly:** identity then encodes a derived value rather than an input,
  so two different seeds legitimately produce different identities for the "same" configuration.
  That is arguably more correct — they *are* different scenarios — but it is a semantic shift and
  should be documented at the key set rather than discovered later.

  **Research supports this over the alternative.** The precedent was traced to `19.2-16-SUMMARY.md`,
  which offers two mechanisms: narrowing the compared key set, or exempting pre-migration
  checkpoints. **Neither is needed here** — and that is the point. Both are guard-loosening
  moves, and this approach makes the checkpoints match because *the value genuinely does not
  change at seed 42*, not because a comparison was weakened. Given anti-pattern 3, achieving the
  property rather than exempting from it is worth a great deal.

- **D-19.4-04 (APPROVED 2026-08-04): populate `E6_COLUMNS`' `depth_range_min` /
  `depth_range_max` from the resolved range, accepting a recorded-only diff on baseline rows.**

  Those columns currently read the config payload directly and are **null on baseline rows**.
  They must be populated from the resolved per-scenario range — otherwise the CSV loses the
  ability to say what depth range was actually used, which is precisely the information whose
  absence caused this bug.

  **Consequence to state up front:** the committed `generalization_sweep.csv` changes at seed 42
  — nulls become values. **No science column moves.** This must be recorded as an expected,
  bounded, non-numerical diff *before* the run, not explained afterwards, or it will read as a
  determinism failure to the next person. The diagnosis confirmed no figure or LaTeX consumer
  reads either column; consumers are `check_rerun_gates.py`, `determinism_probe.py`, and four
  test modules, none of which touch them.

- **D-19.4-05 (APPROVED 2026-08-04): verification scope is code + cheap gates +
  the seed-42 byte-comparison. Defer the previously-failing-seed runs.**

  The diagnosis budgets 6–9 h. Split by what each step actually protects:

  | step | cost | what it protects | verdict |
  |---|---|---|---|
  | 1. Bit-inertness at seed 42, 30 scenarios, `rvec`/`tvec` | ~10 s | **the published numbers** | **in** — non-negotiable |
  | 2. Legality by construction, 500 seeds × {8,12,16} × 3 layouts × 3 spacings = **13,500 scenarios** | **~8 s** (measured, not estimated: 0.593 ms/scenario at n_frames=100, from a 20-seed reduced run) | **the phase goal** | **in** — non-`slow`, `tests/unit/test_experiments_e4.py` |
  | 3. Full unit suite with the test rewrites | ~25 min | regressions | **in** |
  | 4. E6 `--smoke` | ~5–10 min | the path runs end to end | **in** |
  | 5. E6 seed 42 `--force`, byte-compared to the committed CSV | ~96 min | **the published numbers, for real** — step 1 proves the scenes match, not the solves | **in** |
  | 6. E6 at seeds 43 and 47 | ~2.5–5 h | that a fixed sweep *converges*, not merely runs | **defer** |
  | 7. E4 grid at a non-42 seed | ~2–2.5 h | the `n_cameras` axis end to end | **defer** |

  **Why deferring 6 and 7 is defensible:** step 2 proves legality *exhaustively and cheaply* —
  13,500 scenarios in ~8 seconds, versus two scenarios in 5 hours. And convergence off seed 42
  is already evidenced independently:
  19.3 ran E6 at seeds 62 and 28 (naturally legal, no fix), both `{'ok': 14}` with guard count 0
  and `reconstruction_rmse_mm` within ~5% of seed 42 everywhere.

  **What deferring costs, stated plainly:** the `n_cameras` axis is exercised only by
  construction, never end to end, and E4 has up to six legality draws per seed. If steps 6–7 are
  deferred they must be *recorded as deferred* in the SUMMARY with what they would have proven —
  not quietly dropped. With 17 days to 2026-08-21 and Phase 21's dataset regeneration still
  ahead, ~7 h of the ~9 h budget buys evidence for a claim nothing published depends on.

  **D-19.4-06 changes the risk side of this calculation, not the cost side.** With fail-fast in
  place a *broken* fix surfaces in seconds rather than after 74–84 minutes, so the downside of
  attempting steps 6–7 drops sharply. What does not change is the cost of a *successful* run
  (~75–100 min each). So the deferral stands on schedule grounds — but if the calendar loosens,
  step 6 becomes cheap to attempt and is the first thing to add back.

- **D-19.4-06 (USER DECISION, 2026-08-04): the long runs abort on the FIRST failed cell.
  Hard stop and reassess — never burn the full sweep before diagnosing.**

  This is not a preference, it is a defect in the current harness. `run_configuration` wraps both
  scenario builds in one `try` with a blanket `except Exception`, records the failure as a
  `status="failed"` row, and **continues to the next configuration**. That is exactly what cost
  74 and 84 minutes at seeds 47 and 50: all 12 configurations ran, all 12 failed for the same
  reason, and the process **exited 0**. The information needed to stop was available in the
  first second.

  Required behaviour for every verification run in this phase:

  1. **Abort on the first cell/configuration that records `status="failed"`**, before starting
     the next one. Do not complete the sweep.
  2. **Exit non-zero.** A failed run that exits 0 is the root of this whole problem — four dead
     runs were once logged as "complete, exit 0" and caught only by the wall clock.
  3. **Print what failed and why** — configuration key, the exception, and for a clearance
     rejection both the supplied minimum and the derived floor, so the reassessment starts with
     the number in hand.
  4. Opt-out must be **explicit** (e.g. `--no-fail-fast`) for the cases where a recorded failure
     is legitimate — 19.2-21 pre-authorised E4's 16×200 cell to fail as a recorded row, so the
     capability must survive, just not as the default.

  Precedent: 19.3-09 already added a scripted pre-launch abort gate (`22e75ef`). This is the
  same idea moved from before the run to inside it.

  **After this phase's fix, a clearance failure should be impossible by construction** — so a
  failure post-fix means something *other* than the diagnosed defect is wrong, which is
  precisely when continuing is least useful and stopping is most.

- **D-19.4-07 (from research, 2026-08-04): the helper lives in
  `experiments/e4_benchmark_grid.py`, not in the library.**

  `e6_generalization_sweep.py` already imports five grid-family symbols from e4 (`e6:74-80`),
  and `default_xy_extent_for_layout` (`e4:462-484`) is a structurally identical precedent —
  a convenience wrapper E6 uses to derive its scale axis from E4's constants rather than keeping
  a second copy (D-28). Putting the depth-range helper beside it follows the established
  cross-module pattern and keeps the library camera-agnostic.

  **One asymmetry the precedent does NOT carry, and it is the whole bug.**
  `default_xy_extent_for_layout` may re-derive internally with `seed=0` because camera XY
  positions are seed-invariant — its own docstring says so explicitly. `max(water_zs)` is
  seed-**dependent**; it is exactly the quantity that moves. So the new helper **must take the
  caller's already-built `water_zs` as a parameter** and must never rebuild an array of its own.
  A helper that takes `(n_cameras, layout, spacing, seed)` and rebuilds internally would
  reproduce this defect in a new location.

- **D-19.4-08 (found by research — a SEVENTH site the diagnosis's table missed):
  `_BASELINE_EXTENT_ABOVE_FLOOR` (`e6:141`) must be decided deliberately, not inherited.**

  ```python
  _BASELINE_EXTENT_ABOVE_FLOOR: float = GRID_DEPTH_RANGE[1] - GRID_DEPTH_RANGE[0]
  ```

  This is a second, independent consumer of the frozen constant, and it is not in the
  diagnosis's six-site table. Verified in source at `e6:141`.

  Once the floor becomes per-scenario, "the extent above the floor" needs an explicit answer:
  does the extent stay pinned to the **baseline** value (0.818 m, computed once from the
  published geometry), or does it move with each scenario's own floor? D-19.3-07 defined E6's
  scale axis as scaling the *extent above the floor* while holding the floor fixed — so pinning
  the extent to the baseline preserves what the axis measures across seeds, while letting it
  float would make the scale axis measure a different thing at every seed.

  **APPROVED 2026-08-04: pin it to the baseline extent** and document why at the constant. This also
  keeps seed-42 bit-inert, since the baseline extent is unchanged by construction. Flagging it
  rather than folding it in silently, because it changes what E6's scale axis *means*.

### Constraints inherited and NOT reopened

- The published seed-42 E4 grid and E6 sweep are correct and are not re-litigated.
- `board_clearance_floor`'s `margin_factor` is not touched.
- The library must stay camera-agnostic — validation derives from input data, never from
  assumed hardware.
- E6 records a per-configuration failure as a `status="failed"` row rather than raising, so a
  run in which all 14 died still **exits 0**. Check `status` counts, never the exit code. This
  is unchanged by this phase and remains true afterwards.
