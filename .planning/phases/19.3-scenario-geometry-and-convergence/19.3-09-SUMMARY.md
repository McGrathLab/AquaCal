## Task 3 — Post-run review: both findings resolved

Reviewed and approved 2026-08-02. Finding 1 was escalated to a full investigation because the
n_water=1.0 arm is the paper's published non-refractive baseline (submitted §3: "a non-refractive
baseline run in the same optimization framework with n_water = 1.0, so that refraction modeling is
the sole experimental variable"). **Both findings are closed; neither invalidates the run.**

### Finding 1 — RESOLVED: the guard count is bookkeeping, proven twice

**Mechanism.** `7e0cb90` replaced a failed refractive projection's *constant* 100.0 px residual
(zero derivative, an absorbing region) with a pinhole continuation. At `n_water = 1.0` that
continuation is not an approximation — it is exactly the model the baseline is supposed to be, as
the submitted paper itself states (p.4: "When the two media share the same refractive index, there
is no bending at the interface and the model reduces to standard pinhole projection"). `water_z`
therefore has no effect on any projection at n=1, but still gates the NaN mask via `h_q <= 0`.
Pre-guard the large flat penalty acted as a cost *barrier* keeping `water_z` parked; the smooth
continuation removed that barrier, so `water_z` drifted to its upper bound of 2.0 m
(`build_bounds`: `[0.01, 2.0]`), carrying ~47% of corners nominally above the interface.

**Proof A — exact null direction.** Holding every other parameter at ground truth and sweeping
`water_z` from 0.531 to 2.031 m at n=1.0:

```
 water_z (m)   guard count              cost
      1.0310             0    16318366.36810465
      0.5310             0    16318366.36810468
      1.2310           374    16318366.36810466
      1.5310          5572    16318366.36810466
      2.0310         14949    16318366.36810466
```

Cost constant to 13 significant figures (relative variation 2.6e-15); residuals differ by at most
5.7e-13 px. The n=1.333 control over the same sweep moves the cost from 3,768 to 21,156,582 with
residuals differing by up to 109 px — the probe is not blind. Self-consistency bonus: at n=1.333
with `water_z`=2.031 (all observations above the interface) the cost is `16318366.36810466`,
**identical to every n=1.0 row** — direct confirmation the continuation *is* the pinhole model.

**Proof B — end-to-end.** Re-running both arms with `water_z` pinned at ground truth (1.031 m)
via a bounds monkeypatch, closing the loop through triangulation, which is a separate code path:

| depth | non-refr Z-RMSE free | non-refr Z-RMSE pinned |
|---|---|---|
| 1.1 | 49.512 | 49.538 |
| 1.5 | 39.630 | 39.597 |
| 2.0 | 147.248 | 147.209 |
| 2.5 | 248.267 | 248.221 |

Agreement to ~4 significant figures at every depth. Pinning drove the non-refractive guard count
**14,949 → 0** and its optimality **874 → 0.525** (~1600x) while changing no reconstruction number.

**Consequence for the paper: the load-bearing comparison is sound.** The guard cannot touch the
non-refractive arm's numbers. The submitted `~135x` becomes `~128x` (248.2 mm / 1.938 mm) because of
the corrected scenario geometry plus other v1.6.0->v1.8.0 changes — **not** because of a broken
baseline. That decomposition is not attempted here: tonight's run carries all causes at once, and
separating them would need a factorial old-geometry/new-code run.

**Do NOT pin the refractive arm.** It changes that arm materially (2.5 m Z-RMSE 1.938 -> 1.476 mm)
because `water_z` there is genuinely observable and estimating it is the method's contribution;
pinning inflates the ratio to a flattering 168x and breaks the published stable-Z/XY claim
(free: 1.95-2.19, matching §3's ~2.3; pinned: drifts 2.21 -> 1.46). The correct protocol is
**asymmetric** — pin only the non-refractive arm, where the parameter is unobservable.

Recommended for plan 10 / the revision: quote ~128x; adopt asymmetric pinning in E1 so its
non-refractive guard count is honestly 0 and gate 1 passes without an exemption (superseding the
N/A exemption first considered here); record in MF-08 that the baseline was verified invariant.
