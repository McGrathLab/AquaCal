<objective>
Wire `capture_solver_diagnostics` (Plan 19-01) into `joint_refinement`
(`refinement.py:237`, "Stage 3's second pass") and `refine_calibration`
(`point_refinement.py:674`). `joint_refinement` is BENCH-06's second target site (D-11), so
this plan makes its `ftol`/`xtol`/`gtol` explicit with a bit-exact regression guard.
`refine_calibration` is a BENCH-01 site (D-07) but explicitly NOT a BENCH-06 target (D-11) and
is never called from `run_calibration_from_config` (D-14) — its diagnostics surface on its own
`RefinementResult.solver_diagnostics` field, not inside `benchmark.json`.

Purpose: same additive-capture discipline as Plan 19-02, applied to the two remaining
in-scope `least_squares` sites. `point_refinement.py` already passes `ftol`/`xtol`/`max_nfev`
explicitly with its own 200x auto-scaling multiplier (a pre-existing, intentional override —
Research Pitfall 2) — this plan captures that state honestly without unifying it with
`joint_refinement`'s formula or touching its existing tolerance-setting logic.
Output: `joint_refinement` gains `diagnostics_out` + explicit tolerances with a bit-exact
test; `RefinementResult` gains `solver_diagnostics`, populated by `refine_calibration`.

**Both `least_squares` calls in this plan use `method="trf"`** (confirmed live: `refinement.py:241`,
`point_refinement.py:678`). Per 19-RESEARCH.md's corrected Pitfall 5, `njev` is a populated
`int` at both sites regardless of whether `jac` is a callable or `"2-point"` — `None` occurs
only for SciPy's `"lm"` method, which neither site uses. Every test and acceptance criterion in
this plan asserting `njev` must assert it as a populated int, never `None`.
</objective>
